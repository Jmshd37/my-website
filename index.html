ERP research

The people behind ERP performance.

Bachelor’s thesis · Industrial Management

My thesis explores what happens after an ERP system goes live: how employees make it part of their daily work, and how that use may relate to manufacturing performance.

The system is installed. What helps people turn it into everyday value?

01What I worked on

I focused on ERP use after implementation in Uzbek manufacturing companies, connecting academic research with practical questions about everyday work.

02My contribution
  • Reviewed existing research on ERP use after launch.
  • Developed a research framework connecting everyday system use with operational performance.
  • Prepared a survey-based study design for employees who use ERP in their work.
  • Built the study around established questionnaire measures, with English and Uzbek versions to support local participation.
03Why this matters

Production, inventory and procurement teams depend on shared information. This study asks what supports consistent ERP use across everyday operations.

04Who could benefit
  • Manufacturing managers: better-informed decisions about employee support after launch.
  • Employees: greater attention to their everyday system experience.
  • Operations teams: insights that may help improve coordination and information reliability.

University group project · Industry 4.0

Real-Time Capacity Monitoring for Manufacturing

IMGT430 · Spring 2026

A university team project exploring how real-time production data can help Sales make more realistic delivery commitments and help Operations identify capacity constraints earlier.

How can a factory connect incoming orders, machine status and capacity decisions without replacing its existing equipment?

Customer orders→IoT machine data→Capacity engine→Shared dashboard
01The problem we addressed

Our team focused on a common disconnect between Sales and production operations: orders may be captured manually, Sales may not have current shop-floor capacity information, and bottlenecks can become visible only when deadlines are already close.

02Proposed solution
  • Centralize incoming customer orders instead of relying on repeated manual spreadsheet entry.
  • Use IoT retrofit sensors or existing machine data interfaces to detect whether equipment is running, stopped or idle.
  • Collect and clean machine data through an edge gateway before sending it to a shared data environment.
  • Use a capacity engine to combine order demand with available machine capacity and identify bottlenecks.
  • Give Sales and Operations access to the same live dashboard for delivery and capacity decisions.
03Real company visit & confidentiality

The project was informed by an on-site visit to a real manufacturing company. We are grateful to the organization for allowing us to observe its operations and learn from a real production environment. At the company’s request, its name is not disclosed publicly. This portfolio therefore presents only the project concept, process observations and academic analysis, while respecting the company’s confidentiality preference.

04What makes the concept practical
  • Links customer-order information with production-capacity data instead of treating machine monitoring as a separate engineering dashboard.
  • Can work with older equipment through retrofit sensors such as vibration or current-draw monitoring.
  • Supports gradual rollout: begin with one production line, validate the concept, then expand.
  • Uses a lightweight digital representation of available capacity rather than requiring a full 3D digital twin.
05Financial feasibility model

As part of the academic project, our team developed an illustrative pilot business case for one production line. The model estimated an initial investment of about $120,000, a projected net annual benefit of about $68,000 and an estimated payback period of roughly 21 months.

~$120kIllustrative pilot investment
~$68kProjected net annual benefit
~21 mo.Estimated payback

These figures are academic project estimates based on model assumptions; they are not audited results or disclosed financial data from the visited company.

06Implementation approach
  • Phase 1: map the process, select a pilot line and define required data.
  • Phase 2: install sensors, configure the gateway and build the initial dashboard.
  • Phase 3: run the pilot with Sales and Operations feedback and refine the system.
  • Phase 4: expand to additional production lines and add forecasting or automated alerts.
